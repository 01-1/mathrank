    How the scores work

    Each result gets two judgment calls, good to about ±0.3: how much it would matter if it's correct, and how hard it was. Most of these claims haven't been checked by people yet, so read the scores as “if this holds up.”

    Significance

    How much the result would matter. 10 is the Riemann Hypothesis.

    Human result, for scaleSignificanceImpact

    Impact

    Significance on a multiplying scale, so results can be added up: Impact = 10(Significance − 8)/2.

    A solid 8.0 result is 1×. One extra point of Significance is about 3.2× the Impact, and two extra points is 10×. Tile area is Impact unless you size by Difficulty, which uses the same formula with Difficulty in place of Significance.

    The dashed tiles marked “For scale” are human results drawn at the same scale: the Riemann Hypothesis, and Fermat and Poincaré.

    Difficulty

    How hard the problem was for people: how long it held out, how much expert effort went into it, and whether it needed new ideas. Proof length doesn't count. 10 is the Riemann Hypothesis, which is still unsolved.

    Lean proof

    Whether the main theorem was checked by the Lean proof assistant. Partial means only part of the claim, or supporting lemmas. A Lean check only proves the formal statement, and nobody has yet checked those statements against the papers.

    Review and implied results

    Review is what outside experts have said so far. Most results have no review yet.

    The Oct 26 local smoothing result implies two Oct 6 results (3D restriction and Kakeya), so they're drawn inside its tile at their own size. Its score includes theirs, so the rest of its tile is what's new on Oct 26, and they aren't counted again in totals.
